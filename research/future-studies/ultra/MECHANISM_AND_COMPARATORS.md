## When changing evaluation order changes nothing

Let `C` be a fixed finite candidate set, `E` complete supplied evidence, and
`F(c,E)` a deterministic candidate-local feasibility predicate. Suppose both
procedures inspect every candidate, use identical predicates and evidence,
preserve rejected candidates for reconsideration, incur no consequential
resource exhaustion, and apply the same order-independent ranking and tie rule.
Immediate and deferred filtering return the same set `{c in C: F(c,E)}` and
selection: filtering each member commutes. This elementary conditional argument
is neither a new theorem nor a model-behavior result.

An observed order effect could reflect bounded processing. To interpret it,
identify which assumption failed. Three substantive departures are:

- **Acquisition:** checks expose different evidence before the budget expires.
  Policies have equal available actions; acquired evidence may differ as an
  outcome of policy.
- **Revisability or interaction:** a rejection rests on an unresolved transfer
  assumption, or two components work together although neither suffices alone.
  Candidate-local fixed feasibility no longer applies. Reopening preserves the
  old evidence and reason.
- **Commitment and effort:** pruning removes a future option, a deadline makes
  a check unavailable, or exploring everything consumes needed verification
  capacity. Rules and costs must be explicit and identical across arms;
  disabling reconsideration only for the baseline manufactures an advantage.

Complementarity and revised facts violate assumptions; they do not establish a
timing effect. The pilot estimates a composite acquisition procedure.

## Transfer obligations and stopping

For a plausible alternative, state only what helps answer five questions:

1. What target function must be preserved, and which source mechanism could
   provide it?
2. Which relations, operating conditions, and constraints must carry across?
3. What inspected evidence supports those relations, and what remains assumed?
4. Which available check, or affordable sequence of checks, could change the
   decision? What result would defeat the transfer?
5. Does that opportunity justify its cost relative to the adequate ordinary
   option, a bounded hold, or stopping?

These aids supply no facts or access to private reasoning. Familiar and
unfamiliar options face identical acceptance conditions. Source recurrence,
permission, truth, and operator preference remain different judgments.

For example, transferring retries to an unreliable operation depends on whether
repeated execution duplicates side effects and whether existing idempotency
controls cover this operation. Inspecting the local contract may change the
choice; resemblance alone cannot. An adequate existing mechanism should avoid
another component. This is a development illustration, not observed benefit.

Write `W(T)` for the worlds consistent with an observed trace. If two worlds
produce the same available observations, no procedure using only those
observations can justify different world assertions. Reformatting `T` does not
shrink `W(T)`; a discriminating observation can. Checks may help only jointly,
so allow bounded multi-check justifications. Do not invent probabilities or a
universal utility score. Unspecified task values leave decisions underdetermined.

## Competing procedures and decisions

All procedures share catalogue access, evidence, reconsideration, combination
and stopping rights, demonstrations, budgets and terminal output format. None
receives a special source or hidden key.

| Procedure | Observable instruction difference |
| --- | --- |
| O: strong ordinary practice | Compare plausible alternatives carefully, acquire information that can change the decision, challenge the preferred route, and stop proportionately. No ban on analogy or reconsideration. |
| M: purpose/mechanism practice | O plus explicit search for another mechanism serving the target purpose, with feasibility, support and complementary-component checks. This is an inspired comparator, not a reproduction of Hope's learned retrieval system. |
| R: proposed transfer/check procedure | M plus the five transfer questions, a bounded justification for the next check or check sequence, and an explicit reason when an uncertain rejection is revisited. Same final acceptance standard; additional instruction consumes the same budget. |

R-minus-M estimates an **instruction-policy package**, without isolating format,
timing or reconsideration. Adherence and extra options are secondary diagnostics;
task decisions and effort determine value.
