# Public abstract

AI-assisted work inherits decisions made before generation. An answer can be
polished and still feel generic because the system followed a predictable
search path, missed a specialist perspective, flattened source relationships,
failed to compare a meaningful baseline, overlooked an expected absence, or
forgot what happened before.

Pattern Recognition is a proposed discipline for improving those upstream
choices. The Discrimination Layer names the explicit, inspectable,
cost-bounded, and correctable responsibility for deciding what an AI system
should notice, acquire, compare, preserve, question, and allow to influence an
answer. The term refers to technical differentiation among information
candidates and possible next actions; it does not describe social
classification or discriminatory treatment. A layer is a responsibility, not
a mandatory architecture.

The public map keeps six families in view: peripheral signal; source weighing;
velocity and motion; absence and memory; structured patterns; and the learning
loop. Peripheral material is a candidate for inspection, not automatic truth.
Repeated reports are not automatically independent corroboration. Human
judgment, permission, uncertainty, cost, and consequential authority remain
essential.

This is a conceptual framework and a set of testable questions, not empirical
validation or a claim that the six families are newly invented. It may be
implemented as a lightweight decision brief, a richer evidence record, or a
distributed workflow—and may be unnecessary for simple tasks. The invitation
is to inspect the room before asking AI to make the room sound finished.
