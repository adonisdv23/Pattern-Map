# Cover note for mentor review

I’m sending this as a new v16 manuscript because I want to recover the thing I
was trying to say in our original coffee conversation, before the project grew
more technical than the idea needed to be.

The center of gravity is now deliberately broad: an AI answer inherits
choices made before generation. What was searched, missed, compared,
remembered, treated as separate, allowed to influence the context, or stopped
short of can matter as much as the final prose. I’m calling the responsibility
for those choices Pattern Recognition / the Discrimination Layer. The six
families remain visible—peripheral signal, source weighing, velocity/motion,
absence + memory, structured patterns, and the learning loop—but they are
presented as ways of seeing rather than as a product specification.

I moved the common-origin case later and made it one example: nine reports that
trace to one announcement. It is memorable, but it is not the definition of
the project. The Echo Problem remains a separate, unrun research track. The
manuscript also keeps the less glamorous boundaries in view: peripheral does
not mean true, recurrence does not mean independence, provenance does not mean
correctness, access does not mean permission, and an implementation or case
does not validate the idea.

What I would especially like you to challenge:

- Is the upstream-choice thesis the right continuation of the conversation, or
  am I still describing the wrong problem?
- Do the six families feel like a useful map, or do they read as taxonomy
  despite the narrative treatment?
- Does “Discrimination Layer” clarify the responsibility after the plain
  definition, or does the term create too much social or architectural noise?
- Which parts sound like my actual way of thinking, and which parts sound
  over-produced, managerial, or borrowed from a research plan?
- Where should the piece be more ambitious, and where should it make a
  stronger promise to stop, defer, or leave a relation unknown?

This is an owner-review draft, not a finished theory. No reader study, model
run, empirical evaluation, or validation is being claimed. I’d rather have a
mentor disagree with the center of it now than have a polished framework hide
that the center is wrong. If the argument survives your challenge, the next
useful step may be to translate it into builder and agent practices without
making the human thought piece carry all of that machinery.
