# One-page intended-reader summary

## Who the piece is for

The primary reader is an intelligent, technically literate collaborator who uses or builds AI-assisted research and decision workflows but was not present for the conversations that produced this framework. Likely readers include product managers, operators, analysts, researchers, engineers, designers, and evidence-sensitive decision makers.

The piece should also be understandable to a thoughtful non-specialist. It may later interest HCI, information-science, design-science, retrieval, or decision-support researchers, but it is not currently written as an academic paper.

## What the reader should understand after five minutes

1. Many consequential AI errors begin before generation, in the selection and treatment of context.
2. The discrimination layer is a proposed responsibility for making those choices visible, bounded, source-aware, and correctable.
3. `Discrimination` means technical differentiation and judgment, not social classification or unfair treatment.
4. Authority, claim support, independence, relevance, attention, and action are different judgments.
5. The migration-tool example shows how those distinctions change a bounded next action without pretending to discover truth automatically.
6. The framework is a testable synthesis with serious counterarguments, not a validated new science.

## What should remain memorable

- The judgment before generation matters.
- Underweighted information is a starting condition, not a conclusion.
- Recurrence is not independence.
- Provenance is not correctness.
- A decision to search for more information is not a factual conclusion.
- A framework becomes more serious when it names the conditions under which it should lose.

## Desired voice

Direct, curious, concrete, and intellectually honest: a thoughtful memo to an intelligent collaborator. It should retain the practical frustration and visual modularity of v13 without its blanket model claims or product promises. It should not read like a vendor white paper, governance manual, academic imitation, generic AI manifesto, or speculative interface pitch.

## Desired reading experience

The five-minute path should deliver the problem, thesis, map, one example, core distinctions, and open questions. The complete path should deepen each component, show relationships and failure modes, bound the two product cases, and explain what research would be needed.

Readers should never need to inspect claims registers or model-review logs before understanding the piece. Evidence labels and citations should be available without flooding the first fold.

## Questions for the strategic reader

- What central argument do you actually take away?
- Which idea is genuinely useful or memorable?
- Where does the piece first become generic, confusing, inflated, or too cautious?
- What counterargument changes the framework rather than merely adding a caveat?
- What is the smallest structural revision that materially improves comprehension?
- Which claims cannot be verified from the supplied packet?
