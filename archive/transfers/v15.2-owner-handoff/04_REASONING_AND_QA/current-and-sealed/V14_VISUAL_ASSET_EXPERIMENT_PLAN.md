## Decision boundary

V14 already contains the framework’s authoritative semantic structure in HTML: the six-family map, the two-loop relationship figure, the worked-example sequence, text alternatives, and component records. Generated raster imagery may introduce the argument, create continuity with v13, or make a concrete example easier to grasp. It must not become the source of truth for labels, sequence, component count, evidence status, or causal relationships.

The original v13 diagram is not regenerated. The exact recovered PNG is preserved at `archive/v13/pattern-recognition-diagram-v12.png` and should appear as a clearly captioned historical origin map.

## What to create and test

### Role H — Hero editorial image

**Reader job:** feel, before reading the mechanisms, that consequential judgment occurs between a noisy evidence environment and a generated answer.  
**Placement under test:** masthead, after the definition and before the proposition/route cards.  
**Format:** wide landscape, approximately 16:9, crop-safe at desktop and mobile.  
**Non-goals:** no AI brain, robot, chatbot window, magic funnel, surveillance imagery, scales of justice, or claim that the framework is already proven.

#### H1 — Evidence aperture

Create a refined editorial systems illustration for a serious research thought-piece about judgment before AI generation. Wide 16:9 landscape. On the left, a rich but orderly field of heterogeneous evidence fragments: paper edges, observation marks, source trails, faint time-series traces, and differently colored origin points. In the center, a translucent architectural aperture made of layered vellum and fine ink lines visibly separates items into admitted, withheld, unresolved, and sent-back-for-more-evidence paths. On the right, a calm open context field is being assembled, but no answer or computer screen is shown. Include one subtle human-correctable checkpoint through an open movable gate, not a person or hand. Warm ivory paper, dark navy ink, restrained teal as the primary accent, with small coral, violet, ochre, green, and blue details. Editorial information-design aesthetic, tactile paper grain, crisp geometry, generous negative space, sophisticated and non-futuristic. No text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, faces, brains, robots, glowing circuitry, or readable documents.

#### H2 — Braided origins

Create a sophisticated editorial illustration for a research essay about inspecting evidence before AI generation. Wide 16:9 landscape. Several distinct colored evidence tributaries enter from the left. Some apparently separate reports visibly share one upstream origin; other tributaries remain genuinely independent; one fades into an explicit unknown gap. The streams braid through a transparent inspection chamber with reversible side paths for compare, acquire more, withhold, and preserve. The right side resolves into a bounded, quiet workspace made from a few selected evidence pieces, without depicting an output or model. The composition should communicate provenance, common origin, independence, uncertainty, and revisability without a linear conveyor belt. Warm parchment and off-white ground, dark navy linework, teal dominant with restrained violet, coral, ochre, green, and blue accents; elegant scientific-editorial collage, subtle paper texture, strong negative space. No text, letters, numbers, labels, logos, watermarks, people, brains, robots, servers, or generic AI network imagery.

#### H3 — Cartography of attention

Create a museum-quality editorial map-like illustration about deciding what deserves attention before an AI system generates. Wide 16:9 landscape. Depict an abstract evidence terrain with a visible boundary of the currently inspected world, sparse peripheral observations beyond the obvious center, traced provenance routes, clusters that share origins, one missing region, and a movable lens or aperture that changes what enters a bounded context area. Show that peripheral material is only a candidate: some peripheral marks are retained, some rejected, and some remain unresolved. Suggest two timescales with a fast local revision contour and a slower outer learning contour, but do not make a labeled process diagram. Warm ivory stock, navy cartographic ink, teal dominant, restrained coral, violet, ochre, green, and blue accents; precise, calm, tactile, intelligent, substantial negative space. No words, symbols resembling letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, faces, brains, robots, neon effects, or screens.

### Role E — Worked-example editorial image

**Reader job:** understand in one glance how nine apparently separate positive articles may still amount to one originating announcement rather than nine independent confirmations.  
**Placement under test:** beside or immediately before the worked-example sequence.  
**Format:** landscape 4:3 or 3:2, usable as a two-column figure and stackable on mobile.  
**Non-goals:** no real publisher brands, readable headlines, sentiment scoring, or suggestion that common origin makes every claim false.

#### E1 — Nine windows, one origin

Create a clear but beautiful editorial evidence illustration for the example “nine positive articles, one launch announcement,” without rendering any words. Landscape 3:2. Arrange nine visually distinct article-like paper windows across the upper and middle field, each with unique layout marks and slightly different accent colors but no readable text. Fine provenance lines run backward from all nine and reveal that they ultimately branch from one small coral source artifact near the lower left. Add two faint genuinely independent observation nodes elsewhere to show what independent confirmation would look like, but keep them unconnected to the nine. The picture should make common origin obvious only after following the lines; repeated observation is preserved rather than deleted. Warm ivory paper, dark navy, teal, coral, violet, ochre, green, and blue; restrained editorial collage, crisp legible topology, generous margins, subtle print texture. No text, letters, numbers, logos, brand marks, watermarks, faces, screens, or misleading checkmarks.

#### E2 — Echo sheets and watermark

Create a refined editorial illustration of repetition versus independence in evidence. Landscape 3:2. One original announcement is represented as a small coral sheet at the left. It passes through several transformations and becomes nine differently cropped, recolored, and rearranged paper sheets across the frame. A shared translucent watermark-like shape and fine lineage threads reveal their common origin. Nearby, place two truly independent evidence fragments with different material texture and separate origin paths. The composition should say that many mentions are many observations but not automatically many independent confirmations, without implying that the repeated reports are false. Warm ivory background, navy ink, teal framework, small violet, ochre, green, coral, and blue accents; calm premium editorial design, tactile paper, precise connections, crop-safe negative space. No text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks that resemble a real brand, faces, computers, or generic AI imagery.

#### E3 — Provenance constellation

Create an elegant evidence-provenance constellation for a serious research publication. Landscape 3:2. Nine report nodes appear visually separate at first glance, but slender paths converge through intermediate reposting nodes to one originating coral artifact. Two other observation nodes have clearly separate roots, and one node’s origin ends in a deliberate unresolved gap. Preserve every node; distinguish common origin, independent origin, and unknown origin through geometry and material rather than words. Use a warm paper ground, dark navy lines, teal structural arcs, restrained violet, coral, ochre, green, and blue nodes, subtle print grain, and generous whitespace. The result should feel like editorial information art, not a software architecture diagram or decorative network. No text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, arrows with labels, faces, brains, robots, screens, or neon.

## What stays deterministic

- The six-family v14 system map remains semantic HTML.
- The fast evidence-enrichment loop and slower outcome-revision loop remain semantic HTML with visible human checkpoints.
- The component IDs, family names, evidence-status legend, citations, and example steps remain live text.
- Any generated image receives a descriptive caption and alt text that states it is an editorial illustration, not evidence or a system specification.

## Selection rubric

Each candidate is scored from 0–4 on every dimension; a candidate must score at least 20/28 and must receive no zero on conceptual fidelity, non-misleading structure, or crop resilience.

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Conceptual fidelity | Does it express pre-generation judgment, not generic AI capability? |
| Non-misleading structure | Does it avoid inventing a linear or causal topology the paper does not claim? |
| Complementarity | Does it add comprehension or emotional entry rather than duplicate the HTML figures? |
| Editorial fit | Does it belong with the parchment, serif, navy, teal, and six-family accent system? |
| Crop resilience | Does it survive wide desktop, narrow mobile, and print crops? |
| Accessibility support | Can its meaning be summarized accurately in concise alt text and nearby live copy? |
| Craft / distinctiveness | Does it avoid generic “AI network” imagery and obvious generation artifacts? |

## Integration test

1. Review every candidate at native size and at representative desktop and mobile crops.
2. Reject candidates with accidental text, pseudo-logos, ambiguous topology, or implied empirical authority.
3. Select at most one hero and one worked-example image; using none is an allowed result.
4. Add the historical v13 diagram separately with an archival caption and direct link to the live reference.
5. Keep layout stable when images fail to load; preserve readable alt text and captions.
6. Test 1440×900, 720×450, and 390×844, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion behavior, print rendering, lint, build, and rendered-HTML assertions.

## Provenance record for generated files

For each retained candidate, record the role, prompt ID, generator route, generation date, selection score, intended placement, exact file hash, and alt text. Rejected candidates may remain in a local candidate folder for audit but must not be loaded by the production page.
