# One-page claims summary

Status: reader-review aid; the CSV claims register remains canonical.

## Maximum current claim

Some evidence-sensitive AI workflows may benefit from an explicit, inspectable responsibility for deciding what context to acquire, identify, preserve, compare, enrich, admit, withhold, and update before generation. Whether that responsibility improves outcomes enough to justify its cost is an empirical question.

## Claims retained

- Information acquisition and additional computation have costs; value-of-information, metareasoning, and information-foraging research provide established precedents for bounded search and stopping.
- Source assessment and claim support are distinguishable. A source can be authoritative for one narrow claim and weak evidence for another.
- Provenance can establish lineage without establishing correctness.
- Recurrence is not independence; several reports may share one origin.
- One answer may contain atomic claims with different support states.
- More context does not guarantee uniform use of relevant information.
- Human review needs uncertainty, timing, correction, and authority design; merely inserting a reviewer is insufficient.
- V13 historically centered peripheral-signal mining, source weighing, velocity, absence/memory, structured comparison, and outcome learning.
- V13 also explicitly said that less-visible or underweighted material is not necessarily true.

## Claims narrowed or rejected

- **Rejected:** the component mechanisms are new. Retrieval, provenance, evidence synthesis, source credibility, claim verification, value of information, RAG, memory, calibration, and mixed initiative are established prior art.
- **Narrowed:** recurrence across distinct communities is useful context, but community diversity does not prove independent origin.
- **Narrowed:** source track record may inform a domain- and claim-scoped prior; it is not universal trust or claim support.
- **Narrowed:** velocity can prioritize attention only after repeated time-stamped observations and a baseline; it is not a forecast or truth signal.
- **Narrowed:** absence is meaningful only relative to an explicit expected baseline and observation boundary.
- **Rejected:** blanket claims about what “default GPT” can or cannot do. Any baseline must name its model, tools, data access, prompts, date, and budget.
- **Rejected:** the current work proves that non-experts can produce expert-grade output. At most, better evidence handling in bounded tasks is a testable hypothesis.
- **Rejected:** Alpha Solver or Signal Foundry validates the framework.
- **Rejected:** the framework is enterprise-ready, peer reviewed, empirically validated, or complete.

## Proposed contribution

The plausible contribution is not a new mechanism family. It is a boundary-preserving systems synthesis that connects:

1. intent and operational authorization;
2. source/artifact identity and provenance;
3. recurrence, common origin, claims, support, contradiction, and gaps;
4. separate judgments for attention, authority, support, independence, relevance, enrichment, and action;
5. cost-bounded routing and an inspectable context packet;
6. human disposition, versioned memory, and defined outcome feedback.

## Principal falsifiers

The framework is weakened or unnecessary if a simpler cited-retrieval workflow performs equivalently at lower cost; reviewers cannot distinguish its dimensions; common-origin analysis causes more distortion than it prevents; provenance and review overhead do not improve correction or decision quality; outcome feedback worsens calibration or equity; or the title persistently communicates the wrong thesis.

## Current evidence gaps

No systematic review, construct-validation study, controlled comparison, participant study, field pilot, or independent replication has been performed.
