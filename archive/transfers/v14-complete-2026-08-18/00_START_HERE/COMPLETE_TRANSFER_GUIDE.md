## 1. Start here

The package contains a complete v14 practitioner thought piece and systems
framework about an explicit, inspectable responsibility for deciding what
information may influence AI generation. The working name is the
**discrimination layer**, where discrimination means technical differentiation,
selection, and judgment—not discriminatory treatment or social classification.

The final provisional thesis is:

> Some evidence-sensitive AI workflows may benefit from an explicit
> responsibility for deciding what context to acquire, identify, preserve,
> compare, enrich, admit, withhold, and update before generation. This proposed
> responsibility—the discrimination layer—should be inspectable, cost-bounded,
> source-aware, correctable, and revisable. Whether making it explicit improves
> decisions enough to justify its cost is an empirical question.

This is a research-ready design package, not a completed empirical paper. No
benchmark, participant study, preregistration, ethics submission, deployment,
publication, or external release occurred.

## 3. Core reasoning and decision logic

This section records the reviewable rationale behind the work. It is a concise
decision trace, not hidden chain-of-thought.

### 3.1 Why the work moved upstream of generation

The original map emphasized pattern recognition around peripheral signals,
source weighting, velocity, absence, memory, structured patterns, and learning.
The central synthesis became clearer when these were treated as judgments about
the information environment before generation rather than as improvements to
the prose generator itself.

The framework therefore asks what was available, what was authorized, which
records shared an origin, what supported each exact claim, whether another
search was worth its cost, what entered the final packet, and who could correct
the route. The answer remains downstream; the important design object is the
inspectable evidence-to-context decision.

### 3.2 Why the thesis remains cautious

External review suggested making the empirical thesis more assertive. That was
rejected because no matched outcome evaluation exists. A stronger rhetorical
lead—“Make the judgment before generation visible”—was accepted, but the thesis
still says the layer *may* help and that value relative to cost is empirical.

This protects the package from converting architectural plausibility, visual
clarity, or model-review agreement into evidence of effectiveness.

### 3.3 Why the mechanisms are not claimed as new

The research passes found extensive prior art in retrieval, provenance, data
lineage, claim verification, credibility assessment, evidence synthesis,
information foraging, value of information, RAG, memory, mixed-initiative
interfaces, organizational learning, and source attribution. The eleven
responsibilities remain useful as a boundary-preserving synthesis and
evaluation agenda, but a broad mechanism-family novelty claim would be
indefensible.

The first research contribution was therefore narrowed to one controlled,
falsifiable question rather than a test of the entire architecture.

### 3.4 Why origin relation is separated from support and truth

Repeated mentions can remain multiple observations while sharing one known
origin. Common origin does not make a claim false, and separate origin does not
make it true. Likewise, authority, relevance, authorization, support,
independence, consequence, and action priority are not interchangeable scores.

The deterministic receipt demonstrates this separation with:

- `09` observations;
- `01` known cluster;
- `00` supporting origins under the stated packet rule;
- `02` separate comparison roots;
- a preserved `UNKNOWN` state; and
- a human disposition of `HOLD · VERIFY ANOTHER ORIGIN RELATION`.

The receipt is fictional and static. It does not discover provenance, establish
truth, or prescribe a mandatory workflow.

### 3.5 Why the v13 diagram was restored but not made the v14 architecture

At the earlier checkpoint captured in the attached messages, the v13 diagram
was visible on the live site but its original bytes were not yet available.
The then-current v14 omitted it because it represented the older hub-and-seven-
step conception and could not be archived as a verified original. The response
correctly identified the architectural mismatch but also acknowledged that
complete omission lost historical continuity and the most distinctive visual
expression of the original idea.

Later work recovered the exact PNG bytes. The final package therefore preserves
the image unchanged as the **v13 historical origin map**, with SHA-256
`8a8204a05e993e84f2bd9037c59b7beb2ab6b4bca89304e299f66b3961f203ae`.
It is displayed with a live-text transcription of the seven-step strip. The
current semantic family map, two-loop explanation, and receipt remain the v14
representations.

The accompanying HTML is a rendered DOM snapshot from the live page, not the
missing original standalone HTML file. The manifest keeps that distinction
explicit.

### 3.6 Why H1 was archived and E2 retained

Image candidates were judged on conceptual fidelity, non-misleading topology,
editorial fit, crop resilience, accessibility support, and craft.

The H1 evidence-aperture image was visually strong but its lanes and aperture
could imply a one-way pipeline, truth gate, or centralized gatekeeper. It was
therefore removed from the rendered site and PDF and retained only in the
design archive.

E2—nine documents carrying one repeated origin mark—was retained because it
supports the worked example without pretending to be a literal data
visualization or system topology. It is labeled as an editorial illustration,
not evidence.

One final 1200×630 social-card request was made through the current
OpenAI/ChatGPT image-generation route. The interface exposed no exact model
name, so the ledger records the route accurately and does not infer legacy
DALL-E 3. The historical v13 image remains separately identified as the
DALL-E-created anchor.

### 3.7 Why the first paper is narrow

The broad framework combines too many constructs to test credibly in one first
paper. The selected direction is:

> **Oracle Origin-Relation Metadata in One Frozen Model: A Controlled
> False-Corroboration Benchmark**

The proposed model study compares citation-only, rule-only, and byte-identical
rule-plus-stipulated-origin-metadata conditions. The confirmatory F2-versus-F1
comparison uses exact token parity and a frozen model. The primary outcome is
the all-assigned conservative-risk-coded false-corroboration-rate difference.
The safety outcome is fixed-set stipulated-support recall, with a proposed
one-sided 95% lower confidence bound above a frozen `-0.05` non-inferiority
margin. The older `-0.08` value remains planning-only.

This estimates an observable condition effect. It cannot establish internal
reasoning, provenance discovery, human benefit, or validity of the full layer.

### 3.8 Why the HCI study is separate

Model behavior and reader comprehension are different questions. The separate
formative protocol uses eight participants and four presentation conditions to
test whether readers distinguish observations, clusters, supporting origins,
unknown relations, and a human hold decision. Eight participants are adequate
for formative defect discovery, not population-level efficacy, terminology
safety, or accessibility claims.

No participant contact should occur until the appropriate ethics/exemption,
consent, privacy, compensation, recruitment, and accommodation decisions exist.
