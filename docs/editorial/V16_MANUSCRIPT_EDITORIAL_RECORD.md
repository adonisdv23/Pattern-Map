## Voice guardrails used

- Start with an ordinary human experience, then name the technical
  responsibility.
- Prefer “I want to put this on the table,” “imagine,” and “the useful
  question” over theorem or product language.
- Use concrete examples before implementation vocabulary.
- State boundaries as consequences of the idea, not as a wall of opening
  disclaimers.
- Keep the mentor in the room as a thoughtful challenger, not as a ceremonial
  approver.
- Avoid “proves,” “validates,” “demonstrates improvement,” “is novel,” and
  blanket claims about default models.

## Known editorial risks for owner review

1. “Discrimination Layer” remains a high-friction term even with the explicit
   technical definition; the owner should decide whether its precision earns
   the reader cost.
2. The six-family sequence is intentionally visible, but any reader who wants
   a shorter coffee conversation may still experience it as a map or taxonomy.
3. The motion/absence and nine-report examples are fictional illustrations;
   their usefulness and voice fit require owner judgment, not proxy evidence.
4. The essay names implementation levels without supplying the builder or
   agent artifacts; that is an intentional artifact boundary, not a gap to
   fill inside the manuscript lane.
5. Reading times below are word-count estimates. No mentor, cold-reader, or
   public comprehension testing has occurred.
