**The purpose I am preserving.** The project began as a continuation of a coffee conversation with your mentor. It also became useful context for delegating less conventional, better grounded work to agents. Those are related purposes with different reading needs. The six families are a useful repertoire: widen the field; weigh sources for the claim; notice motion against a baseline; inspect expected absence and relevant memory; compare structures; learn from later outcomes. **How to decide whether the next phase is working.** Record value and burden together. Do not reward the method simply for producing more records, sources, caveats, or holds.

| Measure | Practical observation | Guard against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful change | A supported alternative, corrected comparison, or appropriate change in action that the decision owner can explain. | Treating novelty, agreement, or a more polished answer as sufficient. |
| Support and challenge | The material claim is traceable to inspected evidence; a relevant challenge is preserved. | Treating source count or model consensus as corroboration. |
| Appropriate decisiveness | The route proceeds, narrows, holds, or stops for a reason proportionate to the real task. | More refusals or delays being labeled safer by default. |
| Coverage | A material perspective or expected field is considered, or its unavailable/unknown state is retained. | Infinite search, forced diversity, or calling unobserved information nonexistent. |
| Cost to the owner | Additional minutes, review effort, tool/model cost if authorized, and corrections required. | Hiding human work in an apparent token-cost improvement. |
| Learning | A later observation is linked to the original expectation and a scoped update proposal. | Owner approval, preference, or coincidental success becoming factual source weight. |

Two useful cases would justify continuing practical exploration, not an effectiveness claim. **Opportunities beyond the immediate plan.** Three technical directions fit the purpose especially well, provided practical use earns them.

First, select context by its likely relevance to the next decision, not only similarity to a query. A future Signal-to-Alpha packet could retain source pointers and exclusions while foregrounding the few observations most likely to change a bounded recommendation. Later input-removal comparisons could test those claimed dependencies. This is a useful engineering direction and an existing research area; it is not a claim of access to a model's private reasoning.

Second, make observation coverage visible. “Not requested,” “capture failed,” “not authorized,” “expected but missing,” and “checked and absent” lead to different next actions. Signal Foundry already carries much of the relevant state. A practical comparison of how these distinctions change a review decision is more informative than adding another missingness label in isolation.

Third, preserve examples of expert correction as small, source-bound learning cases: what was initially considered, what the reviewer noticed, why a different comparison mattered, what would reverse the recommendation, and what later happened. This can gradually form a useful library for people and agents. Review such updates through existing Protocol Foundry/Council controls. A successful answer's style or an owner's repeated preference should not silently become a universal rule.

Commercially, the most plausible first hypothesis is a narrowly defined service outcome, such as an evidence-backed decision brief or build/reuse assessment. Revenue already supplies the discipline for testing that hypothesis. A standalone “Discrimination Layer” subscription, universal source score, or new receipt product would be premature without a demonstrated customer problem and demand.
